Experience Act I
The starting point

Writing is part of my everyday life. I wanted to explore how a reader’s small action could become part of the story—and how pauses, images and sound could give my words another form.

One decision / Narrative & interaction

Let the reader’s gesture follow the character’s action.

At the end of Act I, two people are packing their desks. An earlier prototype returned to a bus window for its closing interaction. Reviewing it, I found that the gesture broke away from what was happening.

My design decision

Keep the interaction in the classroom. Follow her hands towards the notebook, so that the reader completes an action she has already started. Closing the cover becomes the end of this part of the story.

How the decision became real

Turn a design decision into an actionable requirement.

I used ChatGPT to explore the narrative and interaction, and Codex to implement the prototype. I carried confirmed decisions between them, then reviewed the actual result. The notebook revision shows one such exchange.

Another decision / Sound

Let the music leave, so quiet can arrive.

I wanted the phone scene to become quiet before the music returned with a touch during the ballroom memory. The exit needed its own transition.

Leaving the shop

About 5.8 seconds; footsteps, door and ambience take over.

Checking the phone

The world remains audible while the score is absent.

A touch, then music

A contrasting cue gives the memory its own temperature.

Read how the music approach changed

The original short loop and a later sparse arrangement did not meet my intended effect. I changed the approach and asked for recorded music with clear permission. After auditioning Reverie and Moonlight by Scott Buckley, I directed their cue points and the shop fade. Codex handled sourcing, audio edits and playback checks.
