01 / Starting point

Another way to read a story.

Writing is part of my everyday life. I wanted to explore what happens when a story is experienced through images, pauses and sound—and when a reader occasionally has to do something to continue.

The project began with my fiction. As it took shape, I directed the silhouette style, the order and timing of subtitles, and the moments where sound or interaction could carry the narrative. Reviewing the actual prototype helped me see where the experience departed from the intention.

02 / Decisions

Making the experience match the intention.

Two decisions show how reviewing the prototype changed the design. These are observations from my own reviews, supported by the implemented versions.

DECISION 01

Let the interaction follow the character.

The characters were packing their desks in a classroom, but an earlier version returned to a bus window for a reflection-dragging interaction. During review, I found that this broke the continuity of the scene.

My decision

Keep the interaction in the classroom. Follow her hands towards the notebook and make closing its cover the transition.

What was implemented

An incomplete drag returns gently. Closing the cover triggers a paper sound, a quiet transition and the next line. The gesture is available in the live first act.

DECISION 02

Design the music’s departure, too.

I wanted the phone scene to become quiet, so music returning during the ballroom memory would matter. The shop-exit fade initially felt abrupt. Extending silence alone had not solved the broader musical experience.

My decision

Choose recorded music with clear permission, audition it with the scenes, and make ambience connect the quiet interval to the musical return.

What was implemented

Codex assisted with sourcing, audio edits and playback checks. I reviewed the results and decided on the cues, timing and transition changes.

03 / Collaboration

Turn a reaction into an actionable requirement.

Reviews surfaced concrete issues: awkward arms, objects left behind after being picked up, and actions and sounds happening at different times. I pointed to the specific frame or moment, described the intended behaviour, and asked for similar scenes to be checked.

I directed the experience and made acceptance decisions; Codex handled the code, audio processing and deployment operations. The process documented here is my own prototype review, rather than a study with external users.
